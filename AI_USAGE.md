# AI Usage

AI tools were used throughout this assignment as research and writing assistants.

The tools were not treated as an automatic source of truth. In particular, because the assignment explicitly warns that language models can produce confident but unreliable judgments, I used multiple models for independent evaluation and compared their outputs against the supplied source material.

---

## Tools Used

### ChatGPT

Used for:

- Structuring the Part 1 evaluation methodology
- Reviewing and analyzing the generated question sets
- Assisting with question-level scoring
- Identifying potential redundancy and topic overlap
- Calculating and organizing evaluation metrics
- Comparing independent model evaluations
- Developing the Part 1 recommendation
- Drafting and refining Parts 2 and 3
- Structuring the repository and submission materials

### Gemini

Used as an independent evaluator for the Part 1 question-level scoring.

Its scores were compared against the scoring produced independently by other models.

### Grok

Used as another independent evaluator for the Part 1 question-level scoring.

Its evaluation was compared with the other model evaluations to identify areas of agreement and disagreement.

### Claude

Used to review and improve the written analysis, particularly the Part 2 memo, and to generate alternative observations and interpretations for consideration.

---

## How AI Was Used for Part 1

The question sets were evaluated against the supplied lecture material.

Rather than accepting a single model's evaluation, multiple language models were asked to independently score the questions using the same criteria:

- Groundedness
- Correctness
- Clarity
- Cognitive level
- Difficulty

The resulting evaluations were compared.

Where models disagreed, the underlying lecture material and question wording were reviewed rather than choosing a score simply because one model produced it.

This was particularly important for borderline cases involving:

- Whether a question was fully supported by the lecture
- Whether a question required understanding or application
- Whether a question was genuinely redundant with another question
- Whether a claim was directly stated or required inference

The final analysis was therefore based on the supplied source material and the reasoning behind the scores, rather than blindly averaging model outputs.

---

## How AI Was Used for Part 2

AI was used to help analyze the internal study and draft the memo.

The study results were checked directly against the supplied `study-results.md`.

Particular attention was given to distinguishing:

- Association from causation
- Observed results from interpretation
- Large percentage differences from sufficiently large samples
- Evidence supporting the proposed recommendation from evidence that merely suggests a hypothesis

The graduate subgroup was treated cautiously because of its very small sample size.

AI-generated interpretations were not accepted simply because they sounded statistically or scientifically plausible.

---

## How AI Was Used for Part 3

AI was used to help structure the competitor analysis and identify areas to investigate.

Competitor claims were treated separately from conclusions that could actually be supported by available evidence.

The analysis therefore avoids claiming that a competitor's generated questions are objectively better or worse without directly testing them.

---

## Where AI Helped

AI was particularly useful for:

- Processing and organizing many generated questions
- Identifying potential patterns and overlaps
- Producing independent evaluations
- Surfacing disagreements between evaluators
- Suggesting alternative interpretations
- Organizing the final research structure
- Improving clarity and conciseness of the written work

Using multiple models was especially useful because it exposed cases where a single model could sound confident while making a questionable judgment.

---

## Where AI Was Unreliable

Language models sometimes made confident judgments that were not fully supported by the source material.

For example, models differed on borderline questions involving whether information was explicitly present in the lecture or merely inferable from it. They also differed on whether certain questions represented understanding versus application/reasoning.

There were also cases where a model could interpret a question as unsupported even though the lecture provided enough information to reasonably answer it, or conversely treat a question as grounded when it introduced information that was not actually present in the source.

Because of this, model output was treated as an input to the research process rather than as the final authority.

---

## Reflection

The main lesson from using AI in this assignment was that language models are useful for generating candidate analyses, finding patterns, and challenging an initial interpretation, but they can also produce plausible conclusions that go beyond the evidence.

Using multiple independent evaluations helped expose disagreement, but multiple models agreeing does not automatically make a conclusion correct.

The supplied source material remained the basis for the final judgments.

The most important safeguard was therefore not choosing the "best" AI model, but checking whether each conclusion could actually be supported by the available evidence.
